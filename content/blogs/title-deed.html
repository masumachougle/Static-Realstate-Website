In Dubai, a title deed clearance is mandatory regardless of the reason you are selling, buying, or transferring the property and even settling the mortgage. A title deed that is marked "cleared" indicates that the DLD (Dubai Land Department) has formally accepted the property as being unrestricted, thus enabling a legal and easy transfer of ownership to the new party.

The five major preconditions that one must comply with in order to clear a title deed in Dubai have been discussed below.

1. Valid Identification Documents

To begin the clearance process, one has to first submit valid and current identification. The following are required:

  • Buyer or seller's passport copy
  • UAE residents' Emirates ID
  • Visa page copy (if applicable)

Apart from your identity, these documents are also a necessary requirement for the Dubai Land Department to check ownership and eligibility. The presence of non-existent or invalid documents can cause a delay. Hence, it is always advisable to confirm validity before submission.

2. Original Title Deed Issued by DLD

The original title deed is the unquestionable document that shows the ownership of the property. You are obliged to have:

  • The physical or digital original title deed
  • Exact property details matching DLD records
  • Proof that the property is registered in your name

In case the deed is missing, you will have to request a duplicate before applying for clearance. A clear and valid title deed is the base of all property sales or transfers in Dubai.

3. Developer No Objection Certificate (NOC)

A No Objection Certificate is necessary if the property is situated in a freehold community or within a developer's managed project. The developer like Emaar, DAMAC, Nakheel, Meraas, or Sobha, grants the NOC after verifying that:

  • All service charges have been paid in full
  • There are no outstanding maintenance fees
  • The property is free of violations or pending approvals
  • The owner has fulfilled any developers' obligations

The NOC acts as a guarantee that the developer has no problems with the transfer of ownership and is usually charged at a fee of AED 500 to AED 5,000 depending on the developer and project.

4. Mortgage Release or Bank Clearance Letter

If the property is bought on mortgage, the bank must give the mortgage back officially before the title deed can be cleared. This includes:

  • Paying the remaining loan amount
  • Getting a letter of clearance from the respective bank
  • Bank sending a digital mortgage release to the DLD
  • Erasing the mortgage from the property record

Without this action, the DLD will not be able to register or clear the property for sale, transfer, or refinancing.

5. Dubai Land Department (DLD) Fees Payment

The payment of DLD fees is mandatory in order to clear the title deed. The following items may be included in this fee:

  • Property transfer fees
  • Mortgage release fees
  • Admin or service charges
  • Trustee office fees

The amount of these fees is determined by the property's value and the type of transaction. After all fees have been paid, the DLD will perform the clearance and give out the updated title deed as the final one.

Getting a title deed cleared in Dubai is an easy process if the required documents and approvals are ready in advance. If you make sure to gather the following:

  • Valid ID documents
  • The original title deed
  • A developer NOC
  • Mortgage release / Bank clearance letter
  • All DLD fees paid

Property title deed clearance in Dubai can be made easy to manage, as all the required documents, NOCs, and payment of the corresponding fees would be taken care of in a professional and quick manner.
